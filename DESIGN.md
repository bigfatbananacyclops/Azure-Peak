Meridian (Working Title)

Version: 0.1

This document defines the vision and design philosophy of the project. Before implementing new features, read this document to understand the intended direction of the game.

Vision

Dustline is a multiplayer roleplaying game built on a modified Roguetown/SS13 codebase.

The game is set in a polluted dystopian future along a heavily militarized international border between two fictional nations.

Rounds last approximately 1–2 hours.

The game is NOT intended to be a deathmatch.

Instead, it focuses on:

Border inspections
Logistics
Civilian life
Smuggling
Bribery
Politics
Organized crime
Economy
Asymmetrical conflict

The checkpoint should become the busiest and most important place on the map.

Core Philosophy
1. Civilians are the center of the world.

Cartels and Border Guard are only part of the ecosystem.

Most players should appear to be ordinary civilians.

The checkpoint should never immediately reveal who is a smuggler.

2. Violence is expensive.

Fighting should be dangerous.

Equipment is difficult to replace.

Money is scarce.

Negotiation, deception and bribery should often be better options than shooting.

3. The border never closes.

The United Corporate States depends on the Southern Republic.

Trade cannot stop.

The economy depends on continuous crossings.

This creates believable reasons for constant civilian traffic.

4. Every profession matters.

Every major civilian profession should interact with another profession.

Nobody should feel disconnected from the economy.

Core Conflict

A newly developed synthetic chemical has become the world's most valuable narcotic.

Multiple cartel organizations compete to manufacture and export it.

The United Corporate States attempts to reduce trafficking without shutting down international commerce.

No side can completely eliminate the others.

Gameplay Pillars
Border Inspection

Customs inspect:

Trucks
Cars
Pedestrians
Cargo
Documents

Inspection is one of the game's primary gameplay loops.

Logistics

Legal commerce drives the world.

Truck drivers transport cargo.

Merchants import goods.

Factories require deliveries.

Mechanics require imported parts.

Everything depends on logistics.

Smuggling

Smuggling should blend into legitimate commerce.

Players should prefer disguises, forged paperwork and legal traffic whenever possible.

Illegal crossings exist but should not be the dominant strategy.

Economy

Money is intentionally scarce.

Players should care about:

Losing vehicles
Losing cargo
Paying employees
Buying equipment

Money exists to create decisions, not to make players rich.

Wonder Drug

Only cartel chemists use the advanced production system.

General chemistry should remain intentionally simple.

The Wonder Drug should become one of the signature mechanics of the game.

Systems We Will Keep Simple

To maintain development scope:

Basic food
Simple crafting
Simple chemistry (except Wonder Drug)
Straightforward economy
Limited persistence (initially)

The goal is depth through interaction, not through hundreds of crafting recipes.

Design Rules

When adding a feature, always ask:

Does this create roleplay?
Does this create meaningful choices?
Does this strengthen the economy?
Does it encourage players to cross the border?
Does it fit a 1–2 hour round?
Can a new player understand it?

If the answer is "no," reconsider the feature.

Project Goal

Create a living border town where civilians, merchants, smugglers, politicians, corrupt officials, mechanics, truck drivers, doctors, and Border Guard all depend on one another.

The border itself should be the heart of the game.

Every crossing should tell a story.
